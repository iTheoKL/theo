The Architect

Software, when pursued seriously, is not merely text fed to a compiler. It is mathematical architecture, and an architecture must never be crude.

§ The Quantitative Baseline

"In most fields, being elite is regarded as a ceiling. In quantitative development, it is merely the baseline – the table stakes required to enter the room."

I do not merely write code; I determine what a system must fundamentally be before asking how to assemble it. How its parts interact under extreme strain, whether it preserves its invariants when an adversary deliberately attempts to break it, and how it behaves at the boundaries of its mathematical formulation. That question interests me far more than the ephemeral syntax of any single programming language.

That instinct naturally led me to quantitative development. In ordinary software engineering, being "elite" is celebrated as a high-water mark. In quantitative systems and low-latency market infrastructure – at institutions like Jane Street – being elite is not the ceiling. It is the baseline expectation, the bare minimum required to justify one’s seat at the table.

I. Silicon Sympathy

When capital, risk, and microseconds are bound together, you cannot afford the luxury of hand-waving abstractions. You must possess mechanical sympathy for the hardware: how L1/L2 cache lines invalidate, how vector units broadcast across SIMD registers, and why amortized complexity bounds are an operational illusion during non-stationary market regimes.

II. Coordinates Outside the Terminal

Outside the proof assistant, the terminal, and the compiler, I hold faith with pursuits that demand the same obsessive devotion to craft.

In narrative art – from Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 to Cyberpunk 2077 – I am drawn to worlds constructed with enough structural conviction that choices carry real moral weight. I chose Verso's ending without hesitation. I sent Song So Mi beyond the Blackwall and past the reach of the world she knew, because freedom earned at that price outweighs pragmatic compromise.

In competitive gaming, I follow Valorant esports with the same appreciation for microsecond execution. A championship round turns on a single misread under crushing pressure; the cost of error is as unforgiving as a dropped packet on an optical cross-connect. I fly the banner for EDG, who proved that sustained rigor, discipline, and patience will eventually dismantle any established hierarchy.

And throughout all of it, there is music – an art of pure mathematical proportion, cadence, and human emotion. Whether labeled scholar, quantitative engineer, or craftsman, I hold myself to an uncompromising standard, and I intend to savor every step of the ascent.
