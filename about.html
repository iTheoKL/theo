About The Architect

One eye turned to the Earth, the other to the infinity.

Software, I've come to believe, is a kind of architecture, and a building oughtn't be ugly. I don't merely write code, I ask what a thing should be before I ask how to build it. How its pieces should fit. Whether it holds under strain. Whether it's honest about what it does. That question interests me more than any single language ever could.

That question led me to quantitative development, an environment where intellectual rigour isn't incidental but load-bearing. Where the gap between a sharp idea and a working implementation costs real money, and precision isn't a preference but a condition of survival. What draws me to it, and to Jane Street specifically, is this: in most fields, being elite is the ceiling. Here, it's the floor. The baseline expectation, not the achievement.

Outside the terminal, I keep faith with a few old loves. Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 and Cyberpunk 2077, for the same reason I'm drawn to architecture, worlds built with enough care that their choices carry real weight. I chose Verso's ending. I let So Mi go, out past the world she'd known, because she deserved it more than V. Valorant esports, for proving competition doesn't need a ball or a stopwatch to earn real feeling. A round can turn on a single misread, and it costs exactly as much as it would on any pitch. I fly the EDG banner for a region written off for years, and one run that proved everyone wrong. And I love music, more than is probably reasonable. Call it scholar, architect, or craftsman, I'm still becoming all three, and I intend to enjoy the becoming.
