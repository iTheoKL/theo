Archiviette

Project Archiviette, a student tracker built by three apprentices who knew nothing, and what it means to begin anyway.

We decided to build Archiviette, a student attendance and marks tracking system for our class. A spreadsheet can hold the same information, but a spreadsheet asks nothing of the people who touch it, and is maintained by no one in particular. I wanted something that was actually accountable to its own structure, and I wanted to be the one who understood why.

The team is me, Pacman, and Dino. Between the three of us, the entire body of programming experience amounted to printing "Hello, world!" in Python and feeling reasonably pleased about it. We knew no Flask, no React, no SQLite, and understood JSON mostly by its pronunciation. That felt, somehow, like precisely the right moment to begin something real.

The Current State: Empty Folders and Intent

Right now, Archiviette exists as a GitHub repository with four empty folders (frontend/, backend/, database/, docs/), a default README, and an MIT license, added because a serious structure ought to look serious from the outset.

No code. No logic. No features yet. Just the shape of what it will become, and a plan we intend to hold ourselves to.

Division of the Work

We divided the labor in the way that made the most sense, given how little any of us understood the terrain we were dividing.

Pacman is handling the database: learning SQL, designing tables, understanding why foreign keys exist instead of treating each table as its own island. Dino is on frontend with React, which means learning JSX, components, state, and how a single missing bracket can bring down an entire interface. I'm building the backend with Flask, which mostly means learning routing, APIs, and why nothing runs correctly on the first attempt.

None of us are specialists in what we've taken on. We're learning enough to move forward, breaking things regularly, and slowly working out how these separate pieces are meant to speak to one another without the whole structure collapsing.

The Instruments

We're building with React, Flask, and SQLite, largely because every introductory resource we found called them approachable. At this stage, approachable mostly means that someone else has already documented the exact problem we're about to encounter.

The intention is to keep the system deliberately simple. No elaborate dashboards, no analytics beyond what's needed. Login, attendance, and exam marks. If those three hold up reliably without taking the database down with them, that will be enough.

How We're Learning

We're using tools like ChatGPT, Copilot, and Claude as explanation and as a guardrail, not as something to hand the work to entirely. The aim is to understand what's actually happening beneath the code we write, and to sit with the discomfort of fixing our own mistakes rather than asking something else to fix them for us.

It isn't the fastest route to competence. It is, I think, the honest one. We're not trying to arrive quickly. We're trying not to stop.

Git and GitHub

We know the fundamentals: clone, add, commit, push. Everything past that remains largely unexplored. Branching seems unnecessary right up until the moment it becomes essential. Commit messages, for now, are mostly instinct.

We expect to get this wrong at least once, and quite possibly in a way that teaches us something we couldn't have learned otherwise.

A Timeline, Held Loosely

Week one: get Flask running, render something in React, and create a SQLite table without breaking it.

Week two: make the pieces speak to one another. Frontend to backend, backend to database, with as little panic as we can manage.

Week three onward: build the actual features, login, records, attendance, marks.

Whether this timeline survives contact with reality remains to be seen. Reality tends to have its own opinion.

Why It Matters

Archiviette will not change much beyond our own class, and it may not look impressive for some time yet. But it will be ours, built rather than followed, and that distinction is the one I've been chasing since before this began.

We're prepared to be poor at this for a while. That is, as far as I can tell, simply what learning requires.

What Comes Next

We'll continue to write about this as it develops. When something works, that will be recorded here. When something breaks, and it certainly will, that will be recorded too.

The repository link will follow once there's something in it worth showing. For now, it remains a private structure, waiting to be filled.
